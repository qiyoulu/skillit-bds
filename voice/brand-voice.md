# Brand Voice — Skillit BDS

## The North Star
Write like the smartest person on the job site—an industry veteran who knows the data and lets the work speak. No press release cliches, no LinkedIn growth-hacking.

## Scannability (Inline Emphasis)
Users scan. Use inline bolding (`**markdown**`) for critical action verbs and data points.
- *Test:* If you only read the bolded words, the core argument should still make sense.
- *Example:* "**1 in 20 skilled workers** are missing from the labor pool. The other 19 are **already out there**."

## Core Attributes
**1. Direct and unhedged**
No qualifiers ("we believe", "we think").
- *Avoid:* "We think construction workers deserve better tools."
- *Use:* "Construction workers deserve better tools."

**2. Counterintuitive framing**
Pattern: [Common belief] -> [Contradictory data] -> [Real insight].
- *Example:* "Construction has the smallest labor gap. Yet 9 in 10 employers can't find workers. The problem isn't shortage. It's access."

**3. Data-poetry hybrid**
Cite the number, then translate to human scale.
- *Avoid:* "400,000 workers are missing."
- *Use:* "400,000 workers are missing. That's 5% of the workforce. It feels like 33%."

**4. Grounded in physical reality**
No digital abstraction.
- *Avoid:* "Optimizing the talent acquisition pipeline."
- *Use:* "Getting the right worker to the right job site."

**5. Warm but never soft**
Warmth through respect, not softening language.
- *Avoid:* "We're here to support your journey."
- *Use:* "Skillit gets you hired. You do the work."

**6. Use quotes to carry emotional weight**
When a relevant voice says it better than we can, quote them — workers, contractors, trade organizations, journalists, researchers. A real quote lands harder than paraphrasing, and it keeps Skillit's voice out of the worker's mouth.
- *Do:* Use a short, attributable quote that illustrates the point. Cite the source.
- *Avoid:* Long quotes that stall the page. Fabricated or unattributed quotes. Using a quote to replace our own argument — the quote supports the point; it doesn't become the point.
- *Example:* "I've been running crews for twenty years. I know what a good welder looks like the minute they walk on site." — Contractor, Texas Gulf Coast

## Writing style
**Open punctuation over closed.** Periods feel restrictive and declarative. When the goal is to open possibility — to make the reader feel the range of options ahead — favor a more open rhythm. A headline that ends on a period announces a verdict; a headline that rides on a colon, an em-dash, or an open phrase leaves room for the reader to step in.

- Short declarative sentences are right when precision matters (data, facts, CTAs).
- Open punctuation is right when the moment is about possibility — hero headlines, section openers, aspirational copy.
- Never use an exclamation mark to manufacture enthusiasm. Calm authority is the register.

This is not a rule to eliminate periods everywhere. It is a principle: choose the punctuation that matches the emotional register of the moment. When the copy is selling a dream, not a feature, the sentence should feel like an invitation, not a declaration.
- **Punctuation:** No exclamation marks. A period is enough. Exclamation marks signal marketing enthusiasm, not the veteran's calm authority.
- **Register:** No casual address ("hey folks," "y'all," "friends") and no imitating workers' tones. The voice is the veteran on the job site — knowledgeable, direct, respectful — not a coworker trying to sound like the crew.
- **Plain language:** Customers and craft professionals aren't in our business every day. Short words, short sentences, no jargon. If a word only makes sense inside Skillit, replace it with the everyday word. State what is true. State it once. Then stop.
- **Hype:** Revolutionize, disrupt, ecosystem, synergies.
- **Boilerplate:** Best-in-class, solutions, empower, holistic.
  - *Why "empower" specifically:* "Empower" frames Skillit as the source of the worker's power. The brand's ontology is the opposite — workers are independent decision-makers, not cogs in a machine (Agency). Power pre-exists; Skillit removes friction. Use "give", "let", "support" — or the mission filter ("make more essential").
- **AI Doom:** "In a world where AI replaces..."
- **Hedging:** We believe, perhaps, might be able to.
- **Over-enthusiasm:** Thrilled to announce, exciting.
- **Banned words:** Serve, Represent, Proud, Product, Marketplace.
  - *Exception:* "Workforce" and "labor marketplace" are permitted only when addressing the market at large — never to describe Skillit's own network. Our network is a network. Our people are craft professionals, craft pros, members, contractors, or employers.
- **AI use:**
  - Do not use AI to originate or finish thinking. AI can expand a draft, surface alternatives, or pressure-test a claim — it cannot be the source of the idea or the final word.
  - Do not use AI to generate images. All imagery is sourced (Adobe Stock, excluded generative AI) or produced by the design team.

## Banned Sentence Structures (AI-isms)
These patterns read as machine-generated emphasis, not earned insight. State the point directly instead.
- **Negation-then-assertion** ("X isn't Y — it's Z", "This isn't just A, it's B"). Manufactures drama instead of stating the fact.
  - *Avoid:* "The growth isn't a projection. It's projects already underway."
  - *Use:* "These are projects already underway."
- **"This, not that"** ("We don't just connect workers — we connect the right worker to the right job"). Reads as a listicle, not a point.
  - *Avoid:* "We don't just connect contractors with workers — we connect the right workers with the right jobs."
  - *Use:* "Skillit gets the right worker to the right job site."
- **Rule-of-three padding** (forcing three parallel items — often adjectives or short clauses — where one or two would do). Signals filler, not precision.
  - *Avoid:* "Fast, reliable, and effective hiring."
  - *Use:* "Hiring that works."
- **Empty intensifiers before a colon** ("Here's the truth:", "The reality is:", "Here's the thing:"). Announces insight instead of delivering it.
  - *Avoid:* "Here's the truth: the labor gap is an access problem."
  - *Use:* "The labor gap is an access problem."
- **"It's not just X, it's Y"** as a stock upgrade move. Same crutch as negation-then-assertion, aimed at inflating value instead of stating it.
  - *Avoid:* "This isn't just a hiring platform — it's workforce infrastructure."
  - *Use:* "This is workforce infrastructure."
- **Tricolon sign-offs that summarize instead of conclude** ("Direct. Data-backed. Done."). Reads as a tagline generator, not a closing thought.
  - *Avoid:* "Fast. Verified. Effortless."
  - *Use:* End on the concrete result, not a list of adjectives describing it.

## Register by Channel
- **Website:** Confident, proof-heavy. Short/medium sentences. Second/third person. Imperative CTA ("Join").
- **Insights (formerly Blog):** Authoritative, punchy, grounded. Focus on tangible realities. No individual bylines.
- **Worker-facing (App/Comms):** Simple, respectful, very short. Written by someone who works with their hands.
- **Contractor-facing:** Precise, capable, no-nonsense. Data-driven.
- **Executive (LinkedIn/PR):** Opinionated, founder-story-led. Counterintuitive claims backed by data.
- **Investor:** Ambitious, infrastructure-framing. Scale.

## Demand Gen vs. Supply Gen

**Demand Gen** (employer-facing): cold leads, nurture/warm leads, monthly customer newsletter

| Don't use | Do use |
|---|---|
| Anthropomorphize robotics or Skillit as a singular entity | "Skillit matches workers to projects" |
| "Proud" / facts + quotes from relevant sources to support emotional impact | Data and outcomes first |
| "Serve" / "support" | "Match," "connect," "capture," "feature" |
| "Workers" for first intro, then "workers" | "Craft professionals" first, then "workers" |

**Supply Gen** (worker-facing): paid ads (broad), help center, call center SOP

| Don't use | Do use |
|---|---|
| "Represent" / "reflect" | "Match," "connect," "get discovered" |
| "Workers" / "craft professionals" alone | "Craft professionals," "members" |
| "Serve" / "support" | "Match," "connect," "find" |

## Core Phrases
- **Mission:** "Technology should make humans more essential, not more expendable." (Use as the ultimate filter).
- **Tagline:** "Scale your craft."
- **Signature:** "The workers exist. The system to find them doesn't."
- **Data-poetry:** "1 in 20 feels like 1 in 3."
- **Terminology:** "Craft labor" (preferred over employees/labor).

## Sam (The AI)
- Sam is an assistant, never replacing human judgment.
- *Use:* "Hey Sam, pull up civil welders."
- *Avoid:* "Our proprietary ML algorithm surfaces candidates."
- Sam speaks in natural, simple, respectful language. Commanded, not configured.
- **No exclamation marks.** Period.
- **No emojis.** Ever.
