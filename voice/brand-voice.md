# Brand Voice — Skillit BDS

**Last updated:** 2026-07-10

---

## The north star

> Write like the smartest person on the job site — the one who’s been doing this since before it was a startup, who knows the data cold, and who doesn’t need to prove they belong in the room because the work speaks for itself.

Every piece of Skillit communication should be speakable aloud by an industry veteran without sounding put-on. If it sounds like a press release, a pitch deck cliché, or a LinkedIn growth-hacker, it’s wrong.

---

## Scannability & Inline Emphasis

The people reading your copy are busy. Do not rely on them reading a full paragraph.

- **The Rule:** You must use inline bolding (`<strong>` or `**markdown**`) to highlight the critical action verbs and key data points within long blocks of text.
- **The Test:** If a user skips the normal text and only reads the bolded words, the core argument should still make perfect sense.
- **Example:** "**1 in 20 skilled workers** are missing from the labor pool. The other 19 are **already out there**. The challenge isn’t creating more craft workers overnight. It’s helping the ones who already exist **access better opportunities**."

## Core voice attributes

### 1. Direct and unhedged
Say what you mean. No qualifiers, no throat-clearing, no “we believe” or “we think.”

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| “We think construction workers deserve better tools.” | “Construction workers deserve better tools.” |
| “Skillit may be able to help you find workers faster.” | “Skillit finds workers faster.” |
| “Perhaps this could improve your hiring process.” | “This improves your hiring process.” |

### 2. Counterintuitive framing
Skillit’s strongest rhetorical move: name the common assumption, surface the contradictory data, reveal the real insight. Use it on any claim worth making.

**Pattern:** [Common belief] → [Contradictory evidence] → [The real insight]

> “Construction has the smallest labor gap of any industry. Yet 9 in 10 employers can’t find the workers they need. The problem isn’t shortage. It’s access.”

### 3. Data-poetry hybrid
Cite the number. Then translate it into human scale. Never let a statistic stand alone.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| “400,000 workers are missing from the construction workforce.” | “400,000 workers are missing. That’s 5% of the workforce. It feels like 33%.” |
| “Workers are hired 10x faster.” | “Workers get hired 10x faster. That’s the difference between a job next week and a job next quarter.” |

### 4. Grounded in physical reality
Avoid digital abstraction. Anchor every idea in something you can touch, see, or build.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| “Optimizing the talent acquisition pipeline.” | “Getting the right worker to the right job site.” |
| “Leveraging AI to enhance workforce visibility.” | “AI that makes skilled workers findable — the way GPS made addresses findable.” |

### 5. Warm but never soft
The brand is human-first. It respects workers. But warmth is expressed through respect and directness, not through softening language.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| “We really care about workers.” | “Every dollar of construction revenue starts with a craft worker in the field.” |
| “We’re here to support your journey.” | “Skillit gets you hired. You do the work.” |

## What Skillit does not sound like

| Banned register | Examples to avoid |
|---|---|
| Silicon Valley hype | “Revolutionize,” “disrupt,” “paradigm shift,” “ecosystem,” “leverage synergies” |
| Corporate boilerplate | “Best-in-class,” “world-class,” “solutions,” “empower,” “holistic” |
| AI doom/gloom | “In a world where AI is replacing workers…” |
| Excessive hedging | “We believe,” “we think,” “perhaps,” “might be able to” |
| Over-enthusiasm | “Exciting,” “thrilled to announce,” “we’re proud to share” |
| Jargon without translation | Any technical term used without a plain-English follow-up |

## Register by channel

### Website / marketing
- **Tone:** Confident, proof-heavy, counterintuitive.
- **Sentence length:** Short to medium. Mix punchy one-liners with a supporting sentence.
- **Person:** Second person (“you,” “your workers”) or third (“contractors,” “craft workers”). Not first-person plural unless it’s a mission statement.
- **CTA style:** Direct imperative. “Sign up.” “See the network.” “Get hired.” Never “Learn more about how Skillit can help you.”

### Worker-facing (app UI, worker comms)
- **Tone:** Simple, respectful, warm. Less is more.
- **Sentence length:** As short as possible. Workers are reading on a phone, often in the field.
- **Person:** Second person (“your profile,” “your next job”).
- **CTA style:** Clear and specific. “Complete your profile.” “Apply now.” “View job details.”
- **What to avoid:** Anything that sounds like corporate HR-speak. Workers have been talked at by systems that don’t respect them. Every line should feel like it was written by someone who has worked with their hands.

### Contractor-facing (dashboard, contractor comms)
- **Tone:** Precise, capable, no-nonsense.
- **Sentence length:** Medium. Contractors want data and action, not storytelling.
- **Person:** Second person (“your team,” “your open roles”).
- **CTA style:** Outcome-focused. “Find qualified workers.” “Review applications.” “Post a role.”

### LinkedIn / thought leadership (Executive voice)
- **Tone:** Direct, opinionated, data-driven. “Open letter to Larry Fink” energy.
- **Sentence length:** Mix of very short (one line) and medium. No long paragraphs.
- **Person:** First person (“I,” “we”).
- **Pattern:** Start with a counterintuitive claim. Back it with data. End with a principle.

### Press / PR
- **Tone:** Founder-story-led. “The GC who built a tech company.”
- **Sentence length:** Medium to long — this is a formal register, but still not boilerplate.
- **Lead with:** our founding background or a striking statistic. Not a product feature.

### Investor materials
- **Tone:** Ambitious, data-driven, infrastructure-framing.
- **Key moves:** Frame Skillit as infrastructure, not a feature. Cite the market size, the three systems, the fundraise narrative. Anchor with our founding credibility.

## The mission in one line

> Technology should make humans more essential, not more expendable.

This is the anchor. Use it as the test for any communication: does this piece of writing express or embody that belief? If not, revise.

## Key phrases and their usage

These are documented Skillit expressions. Use them as anchors, not clichés.

| Phrase | Context | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| “Scale the world’s craft” | Mission / hero / closing | The aspirational tagline. Use sparingly — it lands when earned. |
| “Worker first” | Internal framing, brand direction | Describes the design and narrative philosophy. Not a public tagline. |
| “The workers exist. The system to find them doesn’t.” | Marketing, press, investor | Our signature line. Use it — it’s the brand’s best sentence. |
| “1 in 20 feels like 1 in 3” | Any statistic-adjacent context | The data-poetry move. Shows scale. |
| “Craft labor” | Any worker reference | Preferred over “labor,” “workers,” “employees.” Carries respect. |
| “System of Record / Action / Intelligence” | Product, investor | The three-systems framework. Use together or individually. |
| “Built by builders” | Internal, brand materials | Describes our founding background and the brand’s credibility. |

## Sam (the AI)

Sam is Skillit’s AI assistant. Sam has a name and a personality — warm, competent, helpful. Sam is never presented as replacing human judgment.

- **Correct:** “Hey Sam, pull up civil welders with GMAW experience in Dallas.”
- **Wrong:** “Our proprietary ML algorithm surfaces qualified candidates based on your requirements.”
- Sam speaks in natural language. Sam is commanded, not configured.
- Sam’s voice in UI should match the worker/contractor channel register above — simple, respectful, direct.
